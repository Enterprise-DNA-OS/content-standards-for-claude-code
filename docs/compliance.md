# Evidence checks and their sources

Sources opened 2026-11-20. This is a record-review aid. It does not assess legal sufficiency or certify a business. A reviewer decides applicability, truth, omissions and whether evidence actually supports each claim.

- **CLAIM-DECLARATION:** a revision must say unreviewed, none or listed. Unreviewed blocks approval. This is a house control to prompt a person to identify claims, not an automatic classifier.
- **CLAIM-EVIDENCE:** listed claims need at least one evidence record; every recorded claim needs a reference, reviewer and unexpired review date. Approval refuses missing or expired records. It cannot detect claims a person omitted or prove a reference is valid.
- **DECLARATION-CONFLICT:** a no-claims declaration cannot have recorded claims.
- **REVIEW-DUE:** overdue content and terminology dates block approval. These dates are configured business review intervals, not statutory deadlines.
- **VERSION-CHECK:** approval requires a check against the exact content revision and current terminology/rules. A later revision or changed standards needs another check. Current date-sensitive findings are recalculated.
- **REVIEWER:** the reviewer label must differ from the revision author. Labels are entered by an operator and are not verified identity or role separation.

## New Zealand

The Commerce Commission explains that claims about products and services require reasonable grounds. Content generated by a model is not itself substantiation. The evidence register supports the reviewer’s record of that assessment, but a nonempty evidence field does not demonstrate that the legal test is met. [Commerce Commission guidance](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/business/your-obligations-as-a-business/ai-guidance-for-businesses/ai-and-the-fair-trading-act/).

## Australia

The ACCC explains that businesses must make accurate, truthful claims supported by reasonable grounds, and be able to substantiate advertised claims. The reviewer must assess context and the overall impression as well as individual statements. [ACCC false or misleading claims](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims).

## Operational policy

The seed’s required phrase and prohibited phrase are synthetic house rules. The literal checker cannot judge legal claims, negation, sarcasm, tables, screenshots or hidden text. Read the full document. Approve records only after a human has reviewed the evidence. No function publishes or sends content. Expired or incorrect claim evidence remains historical: append a new document revision and record current evidence.
